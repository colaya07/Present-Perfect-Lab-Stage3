01 / LEARN

Grammar essentials

The Present Perfect connects a past action or experience with the present. It does not usually say exactly when the action happened.

THE BASIC FORMULA
have / has+past participle

She has visited three countries. · They have finished their project.

A

Sentence structure

AFFIRMATIVE

I have tried sushi.
He has finished his homework.

NEGATIVE

I haven't tried sushi.
She hasn't called me.

QUESTION

Have you tried sushi?
Has he finished?

Remember: use have with I, you, we and they; use has with he, she and it. En español: have/has cambia según el sujeto.

B

Past participles

Regular verbs usually end in -ed. Irregular verbs have special forms that you need to learn.

Base formPast participleType
workworkedRegular

Watch out: the past simple and past participle can be different: went → gone.

C

When do we use it?

🌍

Life experiences

Experiences at an unspecified time.

I've visited Peru twice.
⏳

Started in the past, continues now

A situation that is still true.

We've lived here for five years.
✨

Recent actions / results

A past action with a result now.

I've lost my keys, so I can't get in.

Also: we can describe changes and achievements up to now. She's learned to drive.

D

Time expressions

ever
at any time in your life — Have you ever flown?
never
not at any time — I've never skied.
already
before now or sooner than expected — I've already eaten.
yet
until now; common in questions and negatives — Have you finished yet?
just
a very short time ago — She's just arrived.
for
duration — for two years
since
starting point — since 2024

For = ¿durante cuánto tiempo? · Since = ¿desde cuándo?

E

Present Perfect vs. Past Simple

PRESENT PERFECT

The exact time is not stated, or the experience connects to now.

I've seen that film.At some time in my life.
PASTNOW
PAST SIMPLE

A finished time in the past is stated or understood.

I saw that film last night.Last night is finished.
PASTNOW

Avoid the Present Perfect with finished-time expressions such as yesterday, last year or in 2020.

A skill that changed everything

Read Maya's story. Then answer the questions using information from the text.

STUDENT STORY
✦↗◎

Small steps.
Big changes.

Reading · A2–B1

Maya's journey

Two years ago, Maya decided to learn how to make short films. She had always enjoyed taking photos, but she had never used a video camera. At first, she found editing confusing, and she almost gave up after her first project received very few views.

Since then, Maya has watched online tutorials and has practised every weekend. She has learned how to plan a story, record clear sound, and edit clips on her laptop. She has also joined a local film club, where she has met people who share her interests.

Maya has made six short films so far. Her most recent film is about a community garden in her neighbourhood. She finished it last month, and the garden's organisers showed it at a local event. Maya was nervous before the screening, but the audience enjoyed it and asked her several questions afterwards.

Maya has not become a professional filmmaker yet, but she has already learned an important lesson: regular practice can help people improve. She has just started planning her next film. This time, she wants to tell a story about young people who are helping their community.
